Solar Halo Phenomenon

The Solar Halo phenomenon (or Sun Halo) is an atmospheric optical phenomenon that creates a glowing ring of light around the sun. It occurs due to the refraction and reflection of sunlight as it passes through countless microscopic hexagonal ice crystals suspended in high-altitude clouds, specifically Cirrostratus clouds. These ice crystals act as natural prisms, refracting light at an angle of approximately 22 degrees to form a large halo ring with a rainbow-like spectrum of colors. Typically, a reddish hue appears on the inner edge closest to the sun, transitioning outward through orange, yellow, and up to a bluish-violet tone on the outer edge. This phenomenon is most clearly visible when the sky is covered with a thin layer of clouds and sunlight shines through them at the appropriate angle. Besides the standard 22-degree halo, it can occasionally occur alongside other optical phenomena such as Sun Dogs or Sun Pillars.

Experimental Steps for Solar Halo Optics Virtual Lab Simulation

  1. Observation and Study of Microscopic Light Path (Micro View)
    • Observe the top simulation screen "1. Micro Edge Refraction" to study the trajectory of white light rays from the sun as they strike and refract through the edge of a hexagonal ice crystal.
    • Observe the dispersion of the light spectrum (red, orange, yellow, green, blue bands) and the actual output refraction angle as it changes according to parameter adjustments.
  2. Adjustment of Experimental Parameters and Variables
    • Adjust Refractive Index of Ice (n): Slide the control bar within the range of 1.250 - 1.400 (default value: 1.310).
    • Select Crystal Type / Refraction Angle (Prism Angle):
      • Select 60° for a 22° halo formation (light passing through side faces of the crystal).
      • Select 90° for a 46° halo formation (light passing through basal faces of the crystal).
    • Adjust Crystal Orientation (Crystal Rotation): Slide the angle control within the range of -25° to 25° (default value: 0°) to study the effect of crystal tilt on the sharpness and blurriness/expansion of the halo ring.
    • Adjust Sun Position (Sun Elevation): Slide the elevation bar within the range of 15° - 85° (default value: 65°) to adjust the sun's height in the sky.
    • Adjust Field of View / Zoom (Field of View): Slide between levels 1 - 3 (Near Zoom, Medium, Wide Angle).
    • Adjust Halo Intensity (Intensity Glow): Slide the intensity range between 50% - 200% (default value: 120%) to modify the brightness and radiance of the halo ring.
  3. Observation of Results and Optical Data Recording (Macro View & Data Analysis)
    • Observe the bottom screen "2. Observer Sky View & Head Rotation" to view the simulated sky, the colorful halo radius (red inner edge, blue outer edge), and the observer's line-of-sight perspective tilted toward the sun.
    • Record calculated results from the "Physics Outputs" panel, including:
      • Minimum Deviation Angle (Min Deviation)
      • Actual Deviation Angle from Tilt (Actual Dev)
      • Apparent Halo Radius (Halo Radius)
      • Crystal Alignment State (e.g., Minimum Deviation Alignment for optimal sharpness, or Tilted State resulting in expanded and faded halos)
