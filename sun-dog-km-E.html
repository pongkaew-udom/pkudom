Sun Dog Phenomenon

The Sun Dog phenomenon (scientifically known as Parhelion or Mock Sun) is an atmospheric optical phenomenon that creates mock suns or bright spots resembling suns flanking the real sun. It occurs due to the refraction and reflection of sunlight as it passes through hexagonal plate ice crystals floating in high-level Cirrus or Cirrostratus clouds. These ice crystals act like natural prisms, deflecting light rays at an angle of approximately 22 degrees. This forms rainbow-like colored spectrum bands on both sides of the sun along a horizontal plane parallel to the horizon. Red usually appears on the inner edge closest to the sun, fading outward to orange and violet. This phenomenon is most clearly observable when the sun is low near the horizon, particularly during early morning or late afternoon before sunset.

Experimental Steps for Sun Dog (Sun Dogs / Parhelia) Virtual Lab Simulation

  1. Observation of Light Behavior at Microscopic Level (Micro View)
    • Observe the top simulation screen "Micro View: Light Refraction through Hexagonal Ice Crystal" to study the path of parallel white light rays from the sun striking flat hexagonal plate ice crystals.
    • Observe light dispersion when minimum deviation occurs at an angle of approximately 21.84° to 22° through the ice crystal, separating white light into various color spectrum bands (red, yellow, green, blue).
  2. Adjustment of Solar and Atmospheric Parameters
    • Adjust Sun Elevation Angle: Slide the control slider between 0° - 45° to observe changes in the sun's altitude in the sky and the increasing distance between the sun dogs and the real sun.
    • Adjust Horizontal Crystal Alignment: Slide the adjustment level from minimum to "Perfect" to see the impact of horizontal ice crystal orientation on the sharpness and shape of the sun dogs.
    • Adjust Cirrus Cloud Density (Ice Crystal Density): Slide within the 10% - 100% range to increase or decrease the amount of ice crystals in the atmosphere.
    • Adjust Brightness and Color Spectrum (Brightness): Slide within the 50% - 250% range to adjust light intensity and color visibility of the sun dogs.
  3. Recording and Analyzing Experimental Results (Macro View & Data Analysis)
    • Observe the "Macro View" display screen which changes according to set parameters, including the real sun's position, 22° halo ring, sun pillar, and both sun dogs.
    • Record calculated data from the "Optical Calculation Data" box, such as the refractive index of ice (range 1.310 - 1.317), minimum deviation angle, and actual distance of sun dogs from the sun.
