Topic: Resistance Measurement using an Ohmmeter

Objectives

  • To study the total resistance measurement of resistors connected in series and parallel configurations using an ohmmeter.

Apparatus

  • Digital Ohmmeter
  • Resistors: 10 units of 100 Ohms resistors
  • Connecting Wires: For constructing series and parallel circuits

Theory

1. Resistors in Series (Series Circuit)

When N resistors of equal resistance R are connected in series, the theoretical total resistance is equal to the sum of all individual resistances, according to the equation:

R_total = R1 + R2 + ... + RN = N × R

Where:
• R_total is the total resistance in Ohms (Ω)
• N is the number of resistors (units)
• R is the resistance of each individual resistor in Ohms (Ω)

2. Resistors in Parallel (Parallel Circuit)

When N resistors of equal resistance R are connected in parallel, the theoretical total resistance decreases, calculated using the equation:

1 / R_total = 1 / R1 + 1 / R2 + ... + 1 / RN ⇒ R_total = R / N

Where:
• R_total is the total resistance in Ohms (Ω)
• N is the number of resistors (units)
• R is the resistance of each individual resistor in Ohms (Ω)

Procedure

  1. Select the desired circuit configuration to test: "Series Connection" or "Parallel Connection".
  2. Adjust the slider to choose the number of resistors (N) to measure, ranging from 1 to 10 units.
  3. Observe the circuit diagram and read the total resistance displayed on the ohmmeter screen.
  4. Click the "Record Data" button to save the theoretical and measured resistance values into the table.
  5. Change the number of resistors (N) or switch the connection mode, then repeat the experiment until completed.
