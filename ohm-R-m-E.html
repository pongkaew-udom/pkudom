Topic: Resistance Measurement using Electrical Circuits

Objectives

  • To study the total resistance measurement of resistors connected in series and parallel configurations using electrical circuits.

Apparatus

  • Digital Multimeter
  • Resistors: 10 units of 100 Ohms resistors
  • Connecting Wires: For constructing series and parallel circuits

Theory

1. Resistors in Series (Series Circuit)

When N resistors of equal resistance R are connected in series, the theoretical total resistance is equal to the sum of all individual resistances, according to the equation:

R_total = R1 + R2 + ... + RN = N × R

Where:
• R_total is the total resistance in Ohms (Ω)
• N is the number of resistors (units)
• R is the resistance of each individual resistor in Ohms (Ω)

2. Resistors in Parallel (Parallel Circuit)

When N resistors of equal resistance R are connected in parallel, the theoretical total resistance decreases, calculated using the equation:

1 / R_total = 1 / R1 + 1 / R2 + ... + 1 / RN ⇒ R_total = R / N

Where:
• R_total is the total resistance in Ohms (Ω)
• N is the number of resistors (units)
• R is the resistance of each individual resistor in Ohms (Ω)

Procedure

  1. Select Circuit Configuration: Choose the desired circuit configuration to test between "Series Connection" or "Parallel Connection".
  2. Set the Number of Resistors: Adjust the slider "Number of active resistors" (N) from 1 to 10 units (where each resistor has a resistance R = 100 Ohms).
  3. Adjust Supply Voltage: Adjust the slider "Power supply voltage" to set the DC power supply voltage in the range of 1.0 V to 30.0 V.
  4. Observe and Read Measurements:
    • Read the potential difference across the resistor network (V) from the voltmeter display.
    • Read the current flowing in the circuit (I) from the ammeter display in both amperes (A) and milliamperes (mA).
  5. Record Data: Click the "Record Data" button to store the experimental values into the data table.
  6. Repeat Experiment: Change variable parameters such as power supply voltage, number of resistors (N), or circuit configuration, then repeat the experiment for comparison.
